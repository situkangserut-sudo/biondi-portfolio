Auth, RBAC & Approval Workflows

VIDA Digital Identity — Self-service Portal

Status: This case study is still being written. The role and process notes below are accurate, but I haven't yet pulled final screens or verified rollout numbers, so there's no results section yet.

Context

Client organizations using VIDA's Self-service Portal needed to manage who on their team could do what: configure authentication settings, approve sensitive changes, and set up role-based access without needing VIDA's internal team to do it for them. I designed the role/permission logic, approval chains, and authentication setup flows for this.

Problem

Access control inside an enterprise identity product is high-stakes by nature — getting it wrong either locks legitimate admins out or over-grants permissions. The existing flows had grown ad hoc as new permission types were added, and there wasn't a consistent pattern for how multi-level approvals (e.g., a change requiring sign-off from more than one role) should work.

My role

  • Designed role and permission logic for client-side admin accounts, including reusable page templates so new permission types didn't require redesigning the pattern each time.
  • Designed multi-level approval chain flows for sensitive configuration changes.
  • Worked on authentication setup flows as part of the broader Verify and Authentication stream, alongside usability testing and VIDA's brand rollout across these flows.
  • Collaborated with engineering on edge cases — partial approvals, revoked permissions mid-flow, and what an admin sees when they lack access to a given action.

Working with a real banking-sector client

Part of this work involved a project with a banking-sector client, where authentication and verification requirements were shaped directly by that client's compliance and security needs. I'm keeping specifics about that client and its internal requirements out of this write-up — the work itself (auth design, usability testing, brand rollout) is represented in the bullets above without naming names.
